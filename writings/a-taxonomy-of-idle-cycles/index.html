A Taxonomy of Idle Cycles

filed 12 aug 2026 · 9 min · all writings

Idle cycles are the dark matter of a datacenter. Nobody has ever seen one directly. We infer them from what fails to add up: the nameplate says one number, the billing says another, and the difference — enormous, embarrassing — hides in the gaps between intent and instruction.

After some years of staring at utilization graphs I have settled on a taxonomy. It is not rigorous. It is not even kind. But it sorts every idle cycle I have ever met into one of five species:

spin
A cycle that votes repeatedly for a future it does not believe in. The lock will become free. The check is cheap. The two facts are unrelated.
prefetch
A cycle spent collecting things for a colleague who will arrive late anyway. The kindest of the species, and the most enabled.
sync
A cycle waiting at a rendezvous point for the last rank to arrive. It is always the same rank. Everyone knows which one.
divergence
A cycle in which the warp agrees to disagree, then does all of it. Democracy in miniature: both branches executed, one answer kept.
review
A cycle spent checking work that was already correct, because the cost of being wrong is priced per incident and the cost of checking is priced per cycle. The pricing is the bug.
Σ idleness ≥ Σ usefulness (2)
observed on every cluster I have touched, at every scale, including this page.

— a utilization graph is a sunset you can bill for —

I keep a folder of utilization graphs that read as sunsets. Operations keeps asking me to delete it. The graphs are four kilobytes each, the folder is called art, and I rest my case.1
