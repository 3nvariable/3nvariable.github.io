The Memory Wall, as a Feeling

filed 30 sep 2026 · 6 min · all writings

You can feel the memory wall before you can measure it. It arrives as a slackness in the profiler: the cores are lit, the tensor units are warm, and the timeline is a hallway of narrow green rectangles with air between them. Nothing is wrong. Everything is waiting.

The textbook says the wall is a ratio, not a barrier — how many operations you can do for every byte you can move. The feeling says otherwise. A barrier you can plan around. A ratio you can only grieve around.

I = F / B (1)
operations per byte: the exchange rate between hope and bandwidth.

I have stopped explaining to people why their model is slow. The honest sentence is not about the model. The honest sentence is: your matmul is fine, the memory is a shared driveway, and everyone on the block owns a truck.1 Nothing in that sentence can be patched, and all of it can be drawn as a roofline.

Notes for the next revision of this note: roofline plots as weather maps; the eight-second rule for prefetch despair; a taxonomy of stalls that reads like the stages of grief, because it is one.
